Privacy Policy

Last updated: August 25, 2026

NYC Stoop ("we", "the app") is built with privacy as the default. This page explains what data the app handles, what it doesn't, and how you can contact us with questions.

Short version: the guide works without an account and keeps your plans on your device. If you sign in, we store your account details. If you use Share, the photos, captions, and comments you post are stored on our servers and shown only to friends you approve — nothing you share is ever public.

Account information (optional)

You can use the guide and planner without an account. If you create one (email, Sign in with Apple, or Google), we store: your email address, a salted hash of your password (never the password itself; none for social sign-ins), your display name if you set one, and a small profile picture if you add one. Accounts exist to identify you to friends in Share and to let you delete your data. You can delete your account in Settings at any time — this permanently removes your account and all your Share content (photos, comments, friendships) from our servers.

Share — photos, friends, and comments

Share is friends-only. There is no public content, no follower system, and no user search — people connect only by exchanging friend codes in person or by invitation.

  • Visibility: photos, captions, and comments you post are visible only to you and friends you have accepted. Removing or blocking a friend immediately hides your content from them (and theirs from you).
  • Photo metadata is stripped: we remove EXIF data — including embedded GPS — from every uploaded image.
  • Location is explicit and optional: if a photo is pinned to a place, that location is a separate field you can see, edit, or remove before and after posting. The app asks your permission before using your device's location, and only uses it to suggest a pin.
  • Moderation: any photo or comment can be reported. Reported content is automatically hidden after multiple reports and reviewed within 24 hours. Photo owners can delete any comment on their own photos.
  • Feedback you send through the app (message + your email if signed in) is stored so we can respond.

What we don't collect

  • We do not track your location in the background, and we never attach location to anything without showing you first.
  • We do not use third-party analytics or advertising SDKs.
  • We do not access your contacts.
  • We do not sell or share your data with third parties.

What stays on your device

The following information is stored in your device's local storage (the iOS equivalent of browser localStorage) so the app can remember your preferences between launches:

  • Venues, works, and sights you've saved with the heart button.
  • Custom places you add via "+ Add" (name, blurb, category, neighborhood, optional address and hours).
  • Your trip plan: which venues are scheduled, day overrides, cuisine choices per meal, notes attached to stops.
  • Your onboarding status (so we don't show it again).

None of this leaves your device. If you uninstall NYC Stoop, this data is deleted with the app. If you want to clear it manually while keeping the app installed, use the "Clear all data" option in Settings.

Third-party services

Map views load tiles from OpenStreetMap and CARTO. When you search for a place to tag a photo, the search text is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim geocoding service. These services log requests for operational purposes (see their privacy policies); we do not send any user identifiers or saved data along with these requests. Our servers are hosted on Vercel, and account and Share data is stored in a managed Postgres database (Neon).

Some external links in the app (ticket booking, restaurant reservations, official venue websites) open in your device's default browser. Once you leave the app, those websites' own privacy policies apply.

Children's privacy

NYC Stoop is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect any information from anyone of any age — including children.

Changes to this policy

If our data practices change in a future version (for example, if we add optional accounts and cloud sync), we'll update this page and bump the "Last updated" date above. Material changes will be flagged in the app's "What's new" notes for the affected release.
